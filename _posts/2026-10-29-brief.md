## Top stories {#top}

- {:#n261029-01} **Leak exposes 41,000 Homeland Security workers** `#breach` — Leaked records include ICE and CBP facility locations, staff phone numbers and members of groups tied to wiretap programs, Palantir and xAI's Grok; Bluesky is blocking links to the archive. *Why it matters:* it puts undercover agents at physical risk and shows how much sensitive data sits in internal directories. [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/massive-leak-exposes-41000-homeland-security-workers-including-undercover-ice-agents/) · [Wired — Bluesky](https://www.wired.com/story/bluesky-blocks-links-to-major-leak-of-dhs-data/)
- {:#n261029-02} **Critical UEFI firmware flaw affects most servers** `#vuln` `CRITICAL` — The bug in UEFI firmware used by most server vendors lets attackers run code early in the boot process. *Why it matters:* code that runs before the OS survives reinstalls and is invisible to EDR, and firmware patching is slow. [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/most-servers-on-the-planet-are-vulnerable-to-a-code-execution-flaw-in-uefi-firmware/)
- {:#n261029-03} **KeyDrift: APT UAT-11473 exploited Cisco SD-WAN** `#threat` — Talos attributes exploitation of CVE-2026-20127, an auth bypass in Cisco SD-WAN Catalyst Controller, to a sophisticated APT deploying the KeyDrift implant. *Why it matters:* network edge controllers keep being the entry point of choice for state actors. [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/uat-11473-keydrift/)

## Security Now cross-check {#security-now}

### SN #1102 — "A Strategic Plug Pull" (27 Oct)

The whole episode was a debrief of last week's PHP2Shell disclosure, which happened before this site started. What we hadn't covered:

- {:#n261029-11} **How PHP2Shell was found** `#vuln` `#ai` — Calif (using an OpenAI model in its "Patch the Planet" effort) and Google's Wiz independently found the bugs; the vulnerable code path had been exploitable in PHP's default configuration for about 14 years. [Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/blog/php2shell-vulnerability-exploitation) · [Transcript](https://www.grc.com/sn/sn-1102.txt)
- {:#n261029-12} **The embargo collapsed — and Chainguard shipped early** `#industry` — CVE IDs leaked two days before the planned release, a PoC video was published by mistake, and the PHP Foundation says Chainguard published fixed images 6+ hours before coordinated release. A case study in why coordinated disclosure struggles at internet scale. [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/php-embargo-dispute) · [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4239342/php2shell-exposes-the-weakness-in-coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure.html) · [Transcript](https://www.grc.com/sn/sn-1102.txt)
- {:#n261029-13} **Exploited before disclosure?** `#threat` — Amazon reported a PHP2Shell attempt in its honeypots dated 17 Oct, days before any leak. [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-php2shell-pre-disclosure-exploitation-2026/)
- {:#n261029-14} **No fix for a huge share of hosts** `#vuln` — Scans show a large fraction of internet-facing PHP runs end-of-life branches that get no official patch; distro backports may keep the old version number, so check package changelogs rather than `php -v`. [Technology Checker](https://technologychecker.io/blog/php2shell-vulnerability) · [Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/blog/php2shell-vulnerability-exploitation)
- {:#n261029-15} **Quest Diagnostics breach** `#breach` — Unauthorized access to Quest's Quanum LSM ordering/results system, discovered 21 Oct; scope of patient records still under investigation. [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/quest-diagnostics-confirms-data-breach-exposed-patient-records)
- {:#n261029-16} **Steve's "strategic plug pull"** `#learning` — His argument that temporarily disconnecting critical systems from the internet may be the right move during the next internet-scale bug. [Transcript](https://www.grc.com/sn/sn-1102.txt)
